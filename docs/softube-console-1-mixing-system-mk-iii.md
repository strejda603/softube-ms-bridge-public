# Foreword

Console 1 is our dream console. It’s our way to create the best in-the-box mixing experience possible, something that feels like working on a large format console, but without the problems that arise when you don’t have access to all the digital features that a modern producer/mixer needs.

![Console 1 Mixing System](https://softubestorage.b-cdn.net/manuals/images/Console_1_Mixing_System_Mk_III/image1.jpg)Console 1 Mixing System

**Hardware is a crucial part of this**. Moving a mouse on screen is not the same as turning a knob or moving a fader and immediately hearing the result, simply because the latter doesn’t require your eyes to be involved. And as soon as the eyes want your attention, listening will lose focus. (Have you ever closed your eyes when you wanted to focus on a mix?)

**Sound quality is essential**. Can you achieve the results you need? If you don’t trust your tools, you lose the flow. Console 1 Mixing Suite includes our dream console, the Core Mixing Suite, which we’ve been working on for ages. It’s made for you. It’s made for fast mixing, broad strokes as well as minute details. Mojo and precision.

Lastly, working on a large format console is fun. **It’s fast and inspiring** because you know your console and you know its limitations. It’s the opposite of scrolling through 200 plug-ins to find the “right” EQ for the kick. Limitations make it easier for you to enter a flow state and become creative. Console 1 is built for flow.

We hope that you will enjoy it, so that we get to enjoy the music you’re making with it!

# First Time with Console 1

## Installation

Please go to [https://www.softube.com/get-started-with-console-1](https://www.softube.com/get-started-with-console-1) for an up-to-date installation procedure!

## The Three Parts of Console 1

The Console 1 Mixing System consists of three parts:

- The Console 1 hardware, which is the **Console 1 Channel**, **Console 1 Compact**, the **Console 1 Fader**, or any combination of those. It's the main controller for everything related to Console 1.
- The **Console 1 plug-in**, the unit that actually process the audio.
- The **Softube On-Screen Display**, the big window that shows settings and information regarding your tracks, as well as being the main application that makes sure that the communication between the plug-ins and hardware units run smoothly.

![The three parts of Console 1, from left to right: the plug-in window, the hardwares, and the On-Screen Display application.](https://softubestorage.b-cdn.net/manuals/images/Console_1_Mixing_System_Mk_III/image2.jpg)The three parts of Console 1, from left to right: the plug-in window, the hardwares, and the On-Screen Display application.

All three parts are necessary for the Console 1 workflow: you control your audio from the hardware, you can see the changes on the On-Screen Display and the audio gets processed in your DAW by the plug-in. It is possible to only use the Console 1 plug-in without the hardware, although the whole experience is designed around the hardware, and that’s where you will notice the biggest improvement to your workflow.

> Always make sure that the Softube On-Screen Display application is running. That’s the app that handles all communication between the hardware, the DAW and the plug-in.

## The Console 1 Workflow

Console 1 is about flow. We want the mixing experience to be like playing an instrument; where you can act and react intuitively and fast, where you can make and correct mistakes or leave room for happy accidents. You will be able to try out ideas quickly and not have to think of much else than your music.

But Console 1 isn’t only for the mixing process, it should also be a natural part of your production workflow. Console 1 was designed with tracking and production in mind and has a low latency (4 samples in 44 and 48 kHz) and low CPU usage that lets you use Console 1 while tracking vocals or recording MIDI with a software synth. The added benefits of having Console 1 set up while you are recording tracks are huge. For example, when the singer asks for more compression, less highs or more reverb, you can quickly engage those sections and adjust it to their liking.

## Your first session

So, you’re new to Console 1? Follow these steps:

1. Place the Console 1 plug-in as the last\* insert on all your tracks and busses. Don’t worry, Console 1 does not use much CPU, and only active sections will process any audio. If it’s on a mono track, insert the plug-in as “Mono to Stereo” or “Stereo”.
2. Setup the sends to your liking. If the DAW supports _DAW Control_, you will be able to control the send volumes from Console 1.
3. Put Console 1 on your returns as well as the main out track.
4. You don’t have to open the plug-in window. It’s enough to just place it on the track to control it through the hardware.

That’s what you need to get started! Next step is to hit play and start working:

1. Click **Display On** (top left button) to open the On-Screen Display.
2. Select which track to work on using the Track Selectors on the top (Console 1 Channel) or bottom (Console 1 Fader) of the unit.
3. **Console 1 Channel:** To engage the EQ section you just need to start turning an EQ knob, and the section will turn on. Press the **EQ On/Off** button below the EQ section to disable it again.
4. **Console 1 Fader:** Grab a fader and control the volumes. Click **Pan** to adjust the panorama from the faders.
5. Select another track and keep on going!

\*) Don't worry, it doesn't _have_ to be last, you can place the plug-in anywhere in the insert chain. But placing it last is a good starting point.

> You should always use the **VST3** version of the plug-in, unless you are using Logic Pro (AU) or Pro Tools (AAX).
> **Use only one single plug-in per track:** Track numbers in the DAW are unique for Console 1, so only one plug-in can be used per track. Having more than one will lead to unexpected consequences.

# General Workflow

Console 1 was built to keep you in the zone while producing and mixing. You hear something, you react. The same knobs in the same spots every time. No unnecessary interaction with “do you really want to… yes or no?” dialogs. Muscle memory and intuitive controls.

There are a lot of features in Console 1. **Don’t worry, you don’t need to know them all**. The most important parts are right at your fingertips, screen printed on the aluminum surface: volume, EQ, compression and sends, etc. Insert Console 1 on all your tracks and adjust these basic parameters from the hardware, and your workflow will be massively improved!

## Fast Workflow with Undo/Redo (Only Channel)

Console 1 doesn’t ask for confirmation when you change things, because that would slow you down. Instead we have **Undo/Redo (Shift + 1/2)** for when you make mistakes, and **A/B/C/D (Shift + 3/4/5/6)** for when you know beforehand that you want to experiment.

![Undo/Redo and A/B/C/D are located at the first 6 track selector buttons.](https://softubestorage.b-cdn.net/manuals/images/Console_1_Mixing_System_Mk_III/image5.png)Undo/Redo and A/B/C/D are located at the first 6 track selector buttons.

If you know just what controls you need at your fingertips every time you start Console 1, the _Default Preset_ feature allows you to save a default signal chain to be loaded each time Console 1 is inserted, and presets allow you to save your favorite chains (or sections) for quick access.

## Scroll/Ok/Cancel

Whenever you need to scroll through a list, for example when loading a section or strip, you use the output volume control **(Volume/Select)** to select items in that list, and to confirm or cancel you use the two buttons just above **Volume/Select**: **Solo/OK** and **Mute/Cancel**.

There is always contextual help at the bottom of each message/dialog.

## More Is More

In general, a clockwise adjustment of a knob means _more_. More compression, tighter Q, more attack time, more gain, etc. There are many different standards on different gear, but in Console 1 the general rule is “more is more”.

## Dual Section’s “On” Buttons (Only Channel and Compact)

To use the dual sections for Shape, EQ, and Compressor, you select and engage/bypass the sections using the same button. For example:

1. Press **EQ** ▶ select the alternate EQ
2. Press **EQ** ▶ again to engage it.

Or:

1. Press **EQ** ▶ select the alternate EQ
2. Turn an EQ knob to activate that EQ.

If you want to bypass the alternate EQ, you press the **EQ** ▶ button while the alternate EQ is selected.

You also use these buttons to select the section when you want to change the plug-in in that section, or change order:

1. Press and hold **Load Strip/Section**
2. Press **EQ** ▶ to load a new plug-in into the alternate EQ slot.

## Using Menus

As mentioned above, you can press and hold **Load Strip/Section** and then click on a section button to immediately load a plug-in to that section. The same type of procedure applies for many of the menus: if there is a logical way to immediately select something using the buttons on the hardware, the menu will allow you to do that. But it is also always possible to use the **Volume/Select** knob to scroll through a list and confirm with **Solo/OK**. Most menus have several ways of interaction, from fast to slow.

Here are some examples. You want to mark a track as favorite:

1. Select the track you want to mark as favorite.
2. Click on **Favorites Assign** ( **Favorites** on Compact)

Or you can do it in the reversed way:

1. Press and hold **Favorites Assign**
2. Select the track you want to mark as favorite
3. Release **Favorites Assign**

The second way is interesting, since it lets you select multiple tracks as favorites:

1. Press and hold **Favorites Assign**
2. The track selectors light up for all tracks marked as favorite
3. Select one or several tracks as favorite
4. Release **Favorites Assign**

## Quick Select

If you want to solo several tracks at once, you can press and hold **Solo** and select the tracks using the Track Selectors.

1. Press and hold **Solo**
2. The track LEDs are colored yellow, and soloed tracks are bright yellow.
3. Select the tracks you want to solo.
4. Release **Solo**.

The same functionality works for **Mute**, but also for the section’s **On** buttons. So if you want to turn off the compressor for multiple tracks, just press and hold the compressors **On** button and select the tracks for which you want to turn on or off the compressor.

At the bottom of the text it will say which features (for example DAW Track Selection) that is currently used in the DAW, and it will also state which Direct DAW Control protocol it is using (for example "Steinberg MIDI Remote" or "Pro Tools PTSCL").

## Function Mode

Used to toggle between different operating modes. At the moment you can change between:

- **Console 1 mode**: The normal way of using Console 1.
- **Pro Tools HUI**: Pro Tools specific HUI mode which enables the user to control track volume from Console 1 Fader _(Console 1 Fader Mk III only)_.
- **Generic MCU**: A simplified and generic MCU (Mackie Control Universal) mode where you have simple control of volume, pan and sends for DAWs that don't support DAW Control _(Console 1 Fader Mk III only)_.
- **Fader MIDI Controller**: Set the Console 1 Fader Mk III to output MIDI CC instead of communicating with Console 1 plug-ins and DAW _(Console 1 Fader Mk III only)_.
- **Apollo Central**: Controlling the plug-ins in Universal Audio’s Apollo Console. Only applicable for UAD-2 Apollo users with a connected Apollo audio interface.

## Bypass

Bypass the entire channel strip on the currently selected track.

To bypass processing on many tracks (only Console 1 Channel):

1. Press and hold **Bypass**
2. Select which tracks to be bypassed using the **Track Selector** buttons.

To bypass the processing on all tracks:

1. Press and hold **Shift**.
2. Press **All (Copy)** to enter “All” mode.
3. Release **Shift**.
4. Press **Bypass** to toggle bypass on all tracks.
5. Release **All**

> If you want to A/B your settings using the **Bypass** function, you probably want to use the internal Output Volume control and not the DAW Volume to match the output gain level. Press **Shift** while changing the **Output Volume** to set the internal output volume.

## Shift button

Shift is used to access secondary functions printed in yellow on the hardware. Some secondary functions, such as the compressor's **Parallel** and **Make-Up Gain**, and the **Width** control in the output section are always available. For other controls it depends on which section has been loaded.

## Load Strip/Section

This functionality is the heart of Console 1 and allows you to customize Console 1 to your needs and sonic desires. From this menu you can either load a full console (strip) or individual sections. For example, swap out the built-in _Core Mixing Suite_ for an _Empirical Labs Trak Pak_ or _SSL SL4000E Series_ console, or switch out the default _FET Compressor_ for a _Tube-Tech CL 1B_ or one of the many UAD-2 compressors that are compatible with Console 1. The availability of different sections and strips depends on what you own and have installed on your computer.

To load a full channel strip:

1. Click **Load Strip/Section**
2. Since “Strip” is already selected, just release **Load Strip/Section** to confirm.
3. Scroll to the strip you would like to load and press **OK**.

If you don’t want to reset the parameter values when you load a strip, press **Shift + OK** instead. _Please keep in mind that it doesn’t always make sense to carry over the settings from one processor to another and that you might get very weird results!_

> Depending on the type of strip you load, it might not occupy all sections of Console 1. Most consoles only have a five sections; filters, shape, single EQ and compressor and an output drive, and will get loaded into the left hand slots (marked with ◀). All other sections will remain as they were.

To load a section:

1. Press and hold **Load Strip/Section**
2. Press the **On** button of the section you want to change or use the **Volume/Select** to scroll down to the section you want to change and press **OK**.
3. Scroll to the selected sub-plugin and press **OK**.

If you don’t want to reset the parameter values when you load the section, press **Shift + OK** instead. _Please keep in mind that it doesn’t always make sense to carry over the settings from one processor to another and that you might get very weird results!_

## Solo Safe

_This section only applies to DAWs without DAW Control where we can’t control their Solo/Mute functionality._

Solo Safe is used to prevent busses from being muted when you solo a track. Console 1’s internal solo will automatically mute all other instances of Console 1, and to prevent Console 1 instances on busses from being muted you need to turn on **Solo Safe** for Console 1 plug-ins on busses.

To set up **Solo Safe**, open Console 1's plug-in window for the instances of Console 1 that is placed on a bus, and press the **Solo Safe** button.

**Solo Safe**: For DAWs without DAW Control, Solo and Mute won’t work on busses unless you turn on Solo Safe for the Console 1 plug-ins that are used on busses.

## Sections

The different processing sections (EQ, compressor, etc) are the things in Console 1 that change the sound, the things that you use to mix your music. In Console 1 Channel Mk III and Compact you have nine sections of processing where you can change sections or load other Softube, UAD or FabFilter plug-ins.

> For specific information regarding the included plug-ins, please see the separate manual for Core Mixing Suite.

1. **Tape/Preamp**: This section is always first and can be used to get tape or preamp flavor on your track. Drive the Input Gain harder to get a more pronounced effect. You can also use Shift + Input Gain to change the headroom of the Tape/Preamp.
2. **Filters**: This section is always in the second position and contains high and low cut filters. These can be used for sidechain filtering as well as for normal filtering.
3. **Dual Shape sections**: The shape sections can contain things that “shape” the sound, such as the Transient Shaper, gate, or utility tools such as a sample delay or de-esser.
4. **Dual EQ sections**: Two equalizers, typically one for precision jobs and one for broad stroke mojo.
5. **Dual compressor sections**: One fast and one slow? One limiting and one pumping? Your choice.
6. **Drive section**: The drive is always last and contains harmonic processing.

The **On** button beneath each section is used to turn the section on/off, as well as selecting that section where you have more than one (Shape, Equalizer and Compressor). To turn on/off a section that isn’t currently visible, you first have to select it and then toggle it's on/off state.

> A section that is turned off doesn’t take any CPU! Even if you put it on all tracks, you only are using DSP based on the sections you decide to turn on.

The **On** button is also used to point to a section. For example in Load Strip/Section, Change Order of a section or sending the filters to a specific section’s sidechain.

For dual sections (Shape, Equalizer, Compressor), the LED on the **On** button shows the current states:

- **On LED** is white: Section is selected and on.
- **On LED** is dimmed white: Section is selected and off.
- **On LED** is yellow: Section is on, but not selected.
- **On LED** is off: Section is off and not selected.

In the On-Screen Display, the selected section has the highlighted title, while engaged sections have a highlighted arrow next to it.

## Group/All (Channel Mk III only)

Group allows you to easily adjust multiple tracks at the same time. Groups are temporary and tracks will be ungrouped just by selecting another track. A common use case is if you want to EQ multiple tracks, for example overhead mics or backing vocals at the same time with the same settings.

1. Select the first track you want to have in the group.
2. Press and hold **Group** while using the **Track Selectors** for all other tracks that should be grouped.
3. Release **Group** to confirm. The tracks are now grouped and changes made will be applied to all grouped tracks at once.
4. When you are done with your grouped changes, click the **Group** button or select another track to ungroup the tracks.

Changes to the **High Cut**, **Low Cut**, **Input Gain**, **Drive**, **Drive Character**, **Pan** and **Volume** will be applied _relatively_, so that you for example can increase the gain by 3 dB on all tracks without affecting the initial gain.

Changes to all other parameters are absolute, basically overwriting the previous settings on the grouped tracks.

> To see the changes applied to all grouped tracks at once, switch over to the “Track Overview” mode using the **Display Mode** button.

**All** has the same functionality as **Group**, but applies to all tracks with a Console 1 plug-in. Access **All** by pressing **Shift + Copy**.

> Please note that **Group/All** does not apply when you change a parameter from a fader on a Console 1 Fader unit. **Group/All** is only active when you change a parameter from a knob or switch!

## Copy (Channel Mk III only)

Copy lets you copy entire tracks or individual sections to another track.

1. Select the track you want to copy from.
2. Press and hold **Copy**.
3. Select

a. The track(s) you want to copy to

b. If you want to copy individual sections from one track to another, for example the “EQ ◀”, select that EQ with the “ **EQ ◀ On** ” button.

4. Release Copy to confirm.


> Please note that DAW parameters, Volume, Pan, Solo, Mute and Sends, are **not** included when you copy settings.

1. Press and hold **Ext. S/C To…**

You need to make sure that the plugin accepts external sidechain, this is typically done in the plug-in window header in the DAW.

## Tape/Preamp

The Tape/Preamp section is the first processing that Console 1 makes. It is loaded with a Tape emulation by default, and as with all sections it is turned off by default to save CPU.

All Tape/Preamp sections have these parameters:

Input GainSets the input level to the Tape/Preamp and also the input level to the rest of the processing in Console 1. Headroom (Shift + Input Gain)Sets how hard the Tape/Preamp is pushed. With **Headroom** at 0 dB, audible distortion typically happens when the input meter reaches above 0 dB. Move the knob clock-wise to push the distortion harder (reducing the headroom) _(Only available on Channel Mk III, Compact and in plug-in.)_Phase InvertSwaps the polarity of the audio.

## Filter

Adjust **High** and **Low Cut** with the two knobs. If it’s possible to change slope of the filters, it is done by holding **Shift** while turning **High Cut** or **Low Cut**.

**Filters To…** disables the filters from the main audio path and instead uses them to filter the sidechain path in one of the dynamics sections. A typical use case would be to filter out noise in the sidechain of a Gate or filtering the low end in the sidechain of a compressor. To use the filters in the sidechain:

1. Press and hold **Filters To…**
2. Select the Shape or Compressor section with its **On** button

![Filters To icon](https://softubestorage.b-cdn.net/manuals/images/Console_1_Mixing_System_Mk_III/image14.png)Filters To icon

## Shape

The Shape sections are typically used for dynamic shaping (Transient Shaper, Gate, etc.) or utilities used to clean up a mix.

For more information, please see the Core Mixing Suite manual.

## Equalizer

The equalizers consist of four fully parametric main bands, as well as two auxiliary bell bands. Each column of knobs has Gain, Frequency and Q, and the two outer bands can be switched between Shelf, Bell and Cut filters as well as change the Slope in cut mode.

> Exact features depend on the current equalizer. Some equalizers only have three or four bands by design, and not all equalizers are fully flexible. The Modern Equalizer in the Core Mixing Suite supports all these features.

For all Console 1 strips, the **EQ Type LED** typically uses the following color indications:

- Green: Bell
- Red: Cut
- Yellow: Shelf

For other equalizers, the meaning of the colors might differ.

## Compressor

The compressor features differ a lot from compressor to compressor, but most have Compression (amount of compression, like a reversed Threshold), Attack, Release and Ratio. Some, like the Bus Compressor, also support the Knee parameter.

Common for all compressors are these built-in parameters:

Wet/DryIncrease to get more parallel processing.Make-Up GainSet compressor make-up gain manually or use the built-in auto make-up functionality. When **Make-Up Gain** is fully counterclockwise it is set to automatic make-up gain. Turn **Make-Up Gain** clockwise to disable the automatic gain and adjust output gain of the compressor from -10 to +40 dB manually.

## Drive

The amount of distortion, saturation or harmonics can be adjusted with the **Drive** knob. **Character** typically makes the drive brighter when turned clockwise, but that depends on which sub-plugin that is loaded into the Drive section.

## Output

The output section with Pan, Volume and Width is "clean", which means that it doesn't impart any color or character from a console emulation (ie. the Volume knob does exactly what it says). If your DAW supports DAW Control, the output section will control the respective functions for that track in your DAW. Otherwise, it controls the functions inside of the Console 1 plug-in.

PanSets the pan of the output signal.WidthSets the width of the output. Turning **Width** counterclockwise will make the bass frequencies mono, turn it fully counterclockwise and the whole output will be mono. From 12 o’clock clockwise it will increase the width of a stereo signal by increasing the gain in the side and lowering it in the mids. Turn it fully clockwise and you have no mono/mid part left. _Width only works when the Console 1 plug-in is inserted as a Stereo or a Mono-to-Stereo plug-in._DAW Volume/VolumeControls the fader volume in the DAW\*, to control the internal output volume in Console 1, press Shift while adjusting Volume.Solo/MuteSoloes or mutes the current track _(If the DAW supports DAW Control)_

In addition to these functions we have five **Volume Knob Mode** switches, with which you can set the function of the **Volume** knob.

- **Volume**: This is the default setting, and the Volume knob controls the output volume.
- **Sends 1-6**: The volume knob controls the send level of the selected send\*.
- **Main Out Volume**: The volume knob controls the output volume of a designated Main Out track. To select a track to be assigned to this button, press and hold **Main Out Volume** while selecting the desired track.

# Console 1 Fader Specific Functionality

If you are familiar with Console 1 in general, and Console 1 Channel Mk III specifically, you will feel at home using the Console 1 Fader Mk III. Many features are shared between Channel and Fader and are described in the sections above. The following sections will only explain the Fader specific features, so if a specific topic isn't included here, please search the rest of the manual for it.

## Faders

Console 1 Fader has ten faders, where each section consists of a 100 mm motorized fader and three buttons.

- **Fader**: Sets the value of the current parameter, typically "Volume". The parameter value and name can be seen on the screen above the fader.
- **Solo**: Solos the current track.
- **Phase**: Press Shift + Solo to phase invert the current track.
- **Mute**: Mutes the current track.
- **Bypass**: Press Shift + Mute to bypass the Console 1 plug-in on the current track.
- **Select**: Press to select the current track.

## Fader Mode

The fader typically controls the output volume of your track, but it is possible to control send levels, pan or any other parameter in Console 1, by using different **fader modes**.

- **Pan**: The fader controls the left-right pan of the track.
- **Send 1-6**: The fader controls the send level (if applicable) of the track. Press **Shift + Send 1-3** to select sends 4-6.
- **Param**: Lets the fader control **any** parameter in Console 1.
- **Volume**: The fader controls the output volume of the track.

### Fader Spill modes

Fader Spill lets you control several parameters (for example Sends 1-6, or different plug-in parameters) on the currently selected track, using the faders. To enter the Fader Spill mode, simply press the Fader Mode button again ( **Param, Send 1-3** ). To exit Fader Spill mode, press the same button again, or **Volume** to enter volume mode.

- **Press Send 1:** Send 1 can be adjusted for all ten tracks.
- **Press Send 1 again**: All active sends (1-6) are displayed on faders 1-6, for the currently selected track. Use the track selectors to select another track to adjust
- **Press Volume** to exit.

The same applies for the **Param** mode. Pressing **Param** again will enter its **Fader Spill** mode.

The parameters are grouped in sections/bands. To change which parameter set is being shown, press and hold **Param** and select the wanted parameter as described below. Press **Shift + Page Up/Down** to bank through the parameters in **Param** mode.

### Param mode

Param mode lets you assign any parameter from Console 1 to the fader, so that you for example can control the Low Cut on all tracks using the faders. This is similar to the quick fader modes for high and low cut, input gain, drive and drive character that was available on the previous generation of Fader.

There are two ways to assign a parameter to the faders.

Assigning a parameter without access to a Console 1 Channel Mk III:

1. Press and hold **Param**
2. Use the red **Pan/Select** knob to scroll through the list of parameters
3. Release **Param** when you have found the parameter.

Assigning a parameter using the Console 1 Channel Mk III

1. Press and hold **Param**
2. All available controls will light up on the Channel Mk III
3. Touch the parameter you want to control. If you want to control a shift-parameter (for example **Wet/Dry** on the compressor, press **Shift** and touch that control.
4. Release **Param** to confirm.

In **Fader Spill** mode, use **Shift + Page Up/Down** to page through the plug-in parameters.

## Fader Trim

**Fader Trim** lets you fine-tune the fader value. Pressing **Fader Trim** will set all faders to their mid position while keeping its value, and the range will be limited to +/- 10 dB for a volume parameter and +/- 20 % for other parameters.

# Screen Reader Functions

The Console 1 accessibility features are only available for macOS users using macOS’s Voice Over or Windows users using Narrator, NVDA or SuperNova. You turn on and off the screen reader for Console 1 with the “Display On” button on the hardware (top left).

Knobs are read when they are touched, while buttons are read when they are pressed, since buttons aren’t touch sensitive.

If you don’t have Console 1 plug-ins inserted on the tracks in your DAW you will only get limited screen reader feedback from the settings menu and similar. Mixing controls and track selectors will be inactive.

If you don’t get any reaction from pressing the display on button, the software might have crashed. Try restarting the “Softube On-Screen Display” application.

Please refer to the manual, as well as the accessible tutorial videos for details on how to use all functions of the hardware and software.

## Special Screen Reader Functionality (Console 1 Channel Mk III)

Shift + Solo: Read input meters in real-time.

Shift + Mute: Read output meters in real-time.

Shift + Compressor On (left): Read gain reduction meter of compressor 1 in real-time.

Shift + Compressor On (right): Read gain reduction meter of compressor 2 in real-time.

Shift + Track 10: Monitor the combined gain reduction at 3 seconds intervals.

## Training Mode

When in training mode, all buttons and knobs read out the parameter name plus the current value of the parameter without changing the value.

The buttons that aren’t linked to a parameter, for example track, page, group, copy, preset and shift, just read their names.

This means that you can’t change track in training mode. The Display On button works as normal and the Mode button turns off training mode.

# Pro Tools HUI Mode

Pro Tools HUI lets you control track volume, solo, mute, pan and sends in Pro Tools from the Console 1 Fader Mk III. It's a different operating mode, and the normal Console 1 functionality (like Fader Trim, Param Mode, etc) is not available.

### First Use

In Pro Tools, set up the following:

1. In **Setup > MIDI > MIDI Input Devices**: Select Console 1 Fader Mk III
2. In **Setup > Peripherals > MIDI Controllers**: Select Type: HUI, Receive from "Console 1 Fader Mk III DAW", Send to "Console 1 Fader Mk III DAW"

In Console 1:

1. Press **Shift + Settings** to enter **Function Mode**.
2. Select **Pro Tools HUI**, and press Solo/OK.

The Fader can now control 8 tracks of Pro Tools, and the text "Pro Tools HUI" along with song position is shown on the tenth display. Console 1 Channel is still operating in "normal" Console 1 mode.

To switch back to Console 1 mode, just press **Shift + Settings** to select **Console 1** again.

### Operation

In Pro Tools HUI mode, the operation of Fader is very different from the normal operating mode. The different functions are described below

- **Pan**: Switches between Pan L ("Pan") and Pan R ("Pan R") on the **Pan** knob
- **Sends 1-5**: Selects Sends 1 to 5 on the **Pan** knob.
- **Param**: Puts the current send on the **Fader**.
- **Page Up/Down**: Selects the next/previous bank
- **Shift + Page Up/Down**: Shifts the bank by one step

Each fader has the following functions:

- **Solo**: Solos the track
- **Mute**: Mute the track
- **Select**: Select the track in Pro Tools. If you have at least one Console 1 instance in the project, using 2024.6 or higher of Pro Tools, you will also select the track in Console 1.
- **Shift + Select**: Record enable the track

> Use at least one instance of Console 1 in your project when you use Pro Tools HUI. That will syncronize track selection between Channel and Fader and also give you full length track names in HUI (Pro Tools 2024.6 and higher)

## Fader MIDI Controller Mode

Console 1 Fader Mk III supports a MIDI Controller Mode, where Fader allows you to control several CC values in order to map to 3rd party plugins, virtural instruments, or even external hardware MIDI devices.

### Getting Started

If you have a single Fader unit:

1. Hold **Shift + Function/Mode**
2. Select **Fader MIDI Controller**
   - On the hardware screen, it should say "Fader MIDI Controller (Included)"
   - On the On-Screen Display, it says "Fader MIDI Controller (All)"

If you have multiple units, it is possible to use a subset of these as MIDI Controllers:

1. Hold **Shift + Function/Mode**
2. Scroll down to **Fader MIDI Controller**
   - On the current hardware, press **Shift + OK** to either include or exclude that hardware from MIDI Controller mode.
   - The text on the hardware screen reads either "Fader MIDI Controller (Included)" or "Fader MIDI Controller (Excluded)".
   - The On-Screen Display will display which unit will be set into MIDI mode: "All", "None", or for example "Fader 1"

When the unit is in MIDI mode, the screen will which MIDI CC that fader controls. Several of Fader’s buttons will also be mappable to CC values. By banking with the page buttons, you will be able to access more MIDI CC values.

Once Fader is in MIDI Controller Mode, mapping to plugins is easy. Typically, you will enter a "learn mode" in your DAW, touch a parameter using the mouse, and then adjust that parameter from Fader. Note that every DAW has a different way in which it maps and handles CC messages. Make sure to check the user manual of your DAW for the best workflow tips in terms of how to assign MIDI CC channels to another product.

### Changing MIDI CC

By default, the faders and CC are ordered sequentially. It is easy to create a custom mapping which will be saved globally.

In Fader MIDI Controller mode:

1. Press **Settings**
2. Touch a fader or press a button to reassign its CC value
3. Use the **Pan** knob to select a CC value
4. Press **OK** to confirm.

This map will be saved in the user library as a JSON compatible text file `Console 1 Fader Midi Map.txt`.

### Hardware Reset (Mk III only)

1. Press and hold **Shift + Display On** for at least 5 seconds to trigger a reboot and firmware reset.
2. The firmware will be reset to factory default, and this is indicated by the text _"Please connect to computer and open the Console 1 app"_ on the display.
3. If it's not already running, start the **Softube On-Screen Display** app.
4. The latest firmware will get installed on the hardware.

### Firmware Upgrade Troubleshooting

If you are experiencing problems when upgrading the Console 1 Channel Mk III or Console 1 Fader Mk III firmware, please try these steps.

1. Disconnect **all** hardware units from the computer.
   - If possible, disconnect other USB peripherals and hubs as well, in case they interfere with the USB power supply (USB-PD).
2. Install the latest version of Console 1 System. This version will contain the latest firmware.
3. Exit Softube Central.
4. Make sure that the Softube On-Screen Display application is running.
   - The icon should be visible in the menu bar.
   - Icon should be gray, since no hardware is connected.
5. Connect the hardwares one at a time, preferably without using a hub if possible.
   - For Console 1 Fader Mk III: the power supply needs to be connected to one USB port, and the computer to the other.
6. The hardware should show "Updating firmware...", and the Softube On-Screen Display should show a progress bar.
7. After upgrading, the hardware will reboot and firmware is upgraded.
   - To confirm firmware version, press "Settings", and scroll down to "About" and press OK.

If this is not working:

1. Swap the USB ports on the hardware. If the right hand USB port is connected to the computer, switch to the left side, and vice versa with the power supply.

If it's still not working:

1. Connect the hardware
2. Press Shift + Display On for at least five seconds, until the hardware reboots.
   - This will force a firmware reset on the hardware.
   - The hardware will upgrade from the original FW version to the latest.

### OMG! My Screens Are All Black!

The units are working, but nothing is showing on the screens.

- Make sure that the **Hardware display brightness** in Settings are set to 100%.
  - Open the On-Screen Display window by pressing **Display On**
  - Press **Settings** on the Console 1 to open settings.
  - Scroll down to **Hardware Settings** and **Hardware display brightness**
  - On that setting, press **Solo/OK** until the value becomes 100%. This should turn on the displays on your unit.
  - Exit by pressing **Mute/Cancel**
